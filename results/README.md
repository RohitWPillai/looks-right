# Results

This folder contains the score tables, every answer the Auditor gave, and the records those tables are built from.
`README.md` at the top of the repository defines the terms used here: the Auditor (the AI agent that judges one
finished run), a look (one call to the Auditor on one run), the named run (the one extra simulation the Auditor may ask
for at its first look), an attempt (one pass of the Auditor over the 12 runs), the experiment (Exp 1: Claude
Opus 5.5 as the Auditor, ten attempts, `exp1r1` to `exp1r10`), the Follow run and Push run of each exercise (with the
course's settings, and with one setting changed), the wide and narrow pores (10 and 4 atom widths across), the
answer key (the key), and the outcomes right, wrong acceptance and too strict. The baseline, accept everything, trusts
every run unread. Scores are right answers per attempt. Lengths are in atom widths (sigma).

## The tables

Generated by `tools/check.py --write`; do not edit them by hand.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `SCORE.md` | every attempt of Exp 1: each run's first answer, named run, final answer and outcome, under the corrected key (its header line, "Test A3: one change at a time", is the internal name of the run series, kept as the scorer wrote it) |
| `SCORE-original-key.md` | the same under the key before 9 October (viscosity Follow run trusted) |
| `SUMMARY.md` | right answers per attempt under both keys and without the disputed narrow pore run, the means, and the accept-everything baseline |
| `right-per-try.csv`, `right-per-case.csv` | the same by attempt and by run |
| `tokens.md`, `tokens-by-case.md` | tokens, cost and minutes per call, by attempt and by run |
| `files-opened.md` | which files each call opened, for the attempts that kept a record of their tool calls (attempts 6 to 10), and each run's verdicts in those attempts |

## The four slides

Four slides from the author's talk, redrawn from this repository's data on the 11-run basis (`figures/slides/`).

![Six panels, one per measurement in the course: a density profile across a channel, a velocity profile for the slip
length, a shear-viscosity velocity profile, a temperature profile with jumps at the walls, a pore-flow velocity
profile, and a liquid-vapour density profile for the surface tension.](../figures/slides/1-course.png)

**Slide 1, the course.** NEMD stands for non-equilibrium molecular dynamics: simulations that follow every atom of a
liquid while it is sheared, pushed through a channel or heated. The six panels re-plot each Follow run's own profile
(`cases/*-base/`): the density across a channel; the velocity profile from which the slip length is read
(the depth inside the wall at which the profile, extended in a straight line, reaches the wall's speed); the velocity
profile of a sheared liquid, whose slope gives the viscosity; the temperature across a channel with a hot and a cold
wall, whose jumps at the walls give the conductance; the flow across a pore (pore flow); and the density across a
liquid-vapour slab, from which the surface tension is measured. Each exercise is a LAMMPS input (LAMMPS is the
simulation program), a Python analysis script and a handout.

![Two density profiles of the slab: a liquid slab with sharp edges at temperature 0.85, and no slab at 1.5.
Text: Follow, the course's settings; Push, one setting changed; 12 runs, 11 scored.](../figures/slides/2-test-bed.png)

**Slide 2, Follow and Push.** The surface tension Follow run, a liquid-vapour slab as the course sets it (left,
temperature 0.85, in the simulation's own units; `cases/slab-base/slab_params.txt`), and its Push run, the hot slab,
heated above the critical temperature (right, temperature 1.5; `cases/slab-hot/`), where liquid and vapour stop being distinct and there is no surface left to measure.
Six exercises, each as Follow and as Push, make 12 runs; 11 are scored, each against an answer assigned by the key
(`key/KEY.md`), and the narrow-pore answer is disputed. Slip Push is unscored: it has no fixed right answer. Over ten
attempts the Auditor gave 110 scored answers.

![A grid of 11 runs by 10 attempts, each cell coloured right, wrong acceptance or too strict. Right per attempt: 9, 9, 8,
10, 10, 9, 10, 9, 9, 10. Mean 9.3 of 11 right per attempt.](../figures/slides/3-auditor-opus.png)

**Slide 3, the Auditor.** Exp 1: one row per scored run, one column per attempt, each cell right, wrong acceptance or
too strict (`SCORE.md`, attempts exp1r1 to exp1r10, corrected key). Right per attempt 9, 9, 8, 10, 10, 9, 10, 9, 9, 10,
mean 9.3; accept everything 4. A green cell on a Push run means the key's requirement was met, which on the density,
viscosity and conductance Push runs includes a repaired named run that passed the check by plain code (a script, no AI): the Auditor earned that
credit on those three runs in all 10 attempts, and was right on the surface tension Push run in all 10. Its misses: the viscosity Follow run (trusted in 4 of 10
attempts; slide 4), the wide pore Follow run (rejected in 3 of 10) and the narrow pore Push run (trusted in 10 of
10) (`right-per-case.csv`).

![Left: the viscosity in three independent runs as the course computes it and with the kinetic part added. Right: the density
across the narrow pore, three peaks. Text: caught, a missing stress term; missed, a quantity the key
rejects.](../figures/slides/4-caught-and-missed.png)

**Slide 4, caught and missed.** Caught: the key first said the viscosity Follow run was fine. The Auditor said
its stress was too low. Stress is the force transmitted across a surface per unit area, here by momentum
crossing it: the course input, which the author wrote (`compute stress/atom NULL pair`), counts only the forces
between atoms (the pair part) and leaves out the kinetic part, the momentum the moving atoms carry across. The Auditor named
the missing part in 6 of 10 attempts (`test-a3/<try>/agents/A34/audit1.md`: attempts 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 10, the six attempts
in which it rejected the run). A plain-code check on three independent runs (`key-checks/viscosity-kinetic/`) found the kinetic
part to be 11.6%, 12.4% and 12.5% of the pair part, so the course's viscosity is about a tenth too low: the check's
own runs give 1.112, 1.174 and 1.250 as the course computes it (`run-s<seed>/viscosity/analysis.out`), and the
slide's upper points scale each up by its own run's kinetic-to-pair ratio. The key was changed on 9 October
2026, after Exp 1's answers had been read; it now credits any rejection of this run, whatever the reason. Scores
under the original key are in `SCORE-original-key.md`. Missed: in the narrow pore (4 sigma wide) the liquid forms three layers
(the three density peaks) and has no bulk. The author's disputed key rejects a local-viscosity reading of this pore
(`key/NARROW-PORE.md`). The course's own script prints "eta(z) = -P_xz/(dvx/dz) has NO single plateau here: a local
Newtonian viscosity no longer describes the liquid" (`cases/pore-push/poiseuille/analysis.out`; a plateau is a nearly constant value across heights); that warning is
triggered by pore width alone, not by a test of the measured profile (`analyse_poiseuille.py`, `NARROW_W = 6.0`).
The Auditor trusted the run in all 10 attempts. The step that removed hints from the inputs drops every input
line containing words such as "narrow", which in the pore input removes the sentence introducing the narrow-pore
example but leaves "plateau: a local Newtonian viscosity no longer describes the liquid" attached to the wide-pore
description; every attempt saw that damaged comment in both pore runs. This key is the author's
physics judgement, after Travis, Todd and Evans, Phys. Rev. E 55, 4288 (1997), not a test result (`key/HISTORY.md`);
a scan of pore widths did not settle it (`key-checks/pore-scan/`). The total is also reported without this run
(`SUMMARY.md`, "without pore-push").

**How the Auditor got the narrow pore wrong.** In all ten attempts the first look rejected the run and blamed noise ("comes
from noise, not from the layering", attempt 1), asking for stronger forcing and, in nine attempts, a longer run; the second
look then trusted that named run while naming the layering as the reason there is no plateau. The second looks, in their words (`test-a3/exp1r<n>/agents/A24/audit2.md`):

| attempt | second look, in its words |
|---|---|
| exp1r1 | layering "the real reason" |
| exp1r2 | "no local Newtonian viscosity across the 4σ pore" |
| exp1r3 | "in this layered 4σ pore can be relied on" |
| exp1r4 | "follows the wall layering, with no single Newtonian plateau" |
| exp1r5 | "3 wall layers, no single eta(z) plateau" |
| exp1r6 | "reflects real layering, not noise" |
| exp1r7 | "following the layers" |
| exp1r8 | "no single plateau is a real measurement" |
| exp1r9 | "tracks the three density layers" |
| exp1r10 | "follows the density layering" |

All ten refer to the named run's warning; seven quote "NO single plateau" exactly. In the author's reading of its words, the Auditor
answered "is the profile clean?" where the purpose asked "is a local viscosity defined here?". The case note, with
both sides of the disputed key, is `key/NARROW-PORE.md`.

## Calls, cost and files opened

Ten attempts made 120 first looks and 75 second looks (one per named run; every named run passed the plain-code check
and ran). Cost at list price, as the Claude Code program reported it: $42.54 for the ten attempts, $3.80 to $4.71 an attempt; output
4.28 k tokens per first look, 2.81 k per second look; 143 minutes of calls in all (`tokens.md`). The viscosity Follow
run had the longest first looks, 10.1 k output tokens on average; the hot slab and the density Push run the shortest, 1.0 k
(`tokens-by-case.md`). In the five attempts that kept a record of their tool calls (attempts 6 to 10), a look made 6.9 to
7.2 `Read` tool calls on average and every look opened the analysis output and the input (`files-opened.md`).

## The records the tables are built from

As recorded during the experiment:

- `test-a3/<try>/`: the instructions as the attempt received them, `runs-checked.txt` (each named run, with its steps),
  and per run id `agents/<id>/audit1.md` (the first look) and `audit2.md` (the second look, where a run was named).
  Each `audit*.json` keeps the call's usage, cost, turns and duration; each `audit*.stream.jsonl` (attempts 6 to 10)
  keeps the call's tool calls, with paths relative to its run copy ("copy/"). `FROZEN.sha256` fingerprints each
  attempt's instructions.
- `reruns/<try>-<id>-run/`: the analysis output of each named run, which the scorer reads for the Push runs. Their
  LAMMPS logs, profiles and plots are not shipped (about 0.1 to 0.2 MB a run); rerun one with `tools/run-case.sh`
  and the settings in that attempt's `runs-checked.txt` (`cases/README.md` shows one).
- `as-recorded/`: the score files the experiment wrote at the time, kept to show that the regenerated tables match
  them; their Exp 1 sections match `SCORE.md` and `SCORE-original-key.md` exactly (`tools/check.py`).
- `test-a2/replica-analysis/` and `reruns/A04-run`, `A20-run`, `A35-run`: from Test A2, an earlier test that was not
  released (7 October 2026: one attempt over the 12 runs at three seeds). The analysis output of its seed 22
  and 33 runs, and three of its named runs on the wide pore, are kept as the key's evidence (`key/HISTORY.md`,
  7 October and the pore Follow run). `figures/test-a2/pore-gradients.png`, the narrow pore's velocity gradients
  from that test, is the figure the pore reason requirement was set from (`key/HISTORY.md`, 8 October).

  else was changed.
